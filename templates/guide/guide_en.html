Sign up and log in

  1. Click “Log in” in the top right corner, then “Sign up”.
  2. Choose a username and a password. An email address is optional, but it makes it easier to recover access.
  3. Pick a time zone (Bishkek by default) and the programming language you like best. You can change both later in your profile.
  4. Click “Sign up!”. You are signed in right away.
{{ shot('register', 'Sign-up form') }}

The interface language (Russian, Kyrgyz, English) and the dark theme are switched in the top bar. As a guest you can use the compiler, browse problems and play quizzes; submissions, courses and certificates need an account.

Problems and submissions

  1. Open “Problems”. The list can be filtered by topic and difficulty and searched by name.
  2. Open a problem and read the statement: input, output, an example, and the time and memory limits.
  3. Click “Solve in the editor” (you write code right on the site) or “Submit solution” (paste ready code).
  4. Choose a language, write the solution and click “Run” to try it on the example or on your own input.
  5. Click “Submit”. The judge checks the solution against hidden tests.
{{ shot('problem-page', 'Problem page: statement on the left, points and limits on the right') }} {{ shot('submit-editor', 'Solution editor: statement, code and a test run') }}

Result and points

Each problem gives up to 100 points. Points follow the share of tests passed, so even a partial solution earns something, while a full one gets all 100 and the verdict “Accepted”. Your submissions are listed under “Submissions”, tab “Mine”.

{{ shot('submissions-mine', 'My submissions: verdict, points, time and memory') }}
If a solution fails, open the submission: it shows which test went wrong (wrong answer, time limit exceeded, runtime error).

Compiler

The “Compiler” section is an online development environment. It works without an account; sign in to keep your projects and files.

  1. Choose a language at the top right: C, C++, C#, F#, Java, Python, JavaScript, Go, Kotlin, Pascal or Scratch.
  2. Write code. The editor suggests names and keywords as you type. To open the list by hand, press Ctrl+Space.
  3. Click “Run”. The program output appears in the console below.
  4. If the program waits for input, type it right in the console (“Interactive console” mode), or switch to “Text” and paste all the input at once.
{{ shot('compiler-autocomplete', 'Suggestions while typing') }} {{ shot('compiler-run', 'Running a program: output, time and memory') }}

“Share” makes a link to your file, “Format” tidies the code, “Save” stores the project in your account. The left panel lets you add files and folders.

Olympiads

  1. Open “Contests”: running and upcoming olympiads are on top, past ones below.
  2. Open an olympiad and click the join button. For closed olympiads the teacher gives you a login and password or a link.
  3. Once it starts, the problems appear on its page. Solve them as usual: in the editor or by submitting code.
  4. The scoreboard updates during the olympiad; the final results open when it ends.
{{ shot('contests', 'List of olympiads') }}
Anti-cheating is on during olympiads: switching to other tabs and pasting foreign code are recorded. Solve the problems on your own.

Play a quiz

A quiz runs during a class: the host shows questions on the screen and you answer on your phone.

  1. On your phone open olymp.oshsu.kg/quiz/join, or point the camera at the QR code on the host’s screen.
  2. Enter the game PIN (six digits).
  3. Type a nickname and pick an avatar. Click “Play”.
  4. When the host starts the game, choose your answer on the phone. The faster you answer correctly, the more points you get.

The quiz catalog also holds ready-made quizzes you can play alone: open any of them and click “Play solo”.

Create and host a quiz

  1. In “Quiz” open “My quizzes” and create a new one. You need to be signed in.
  2. Give the quiz a title. In “Settings” you choose the access (“Public” or “By link”), the look and the cover.
  3. Click “+ Question” and choose a type.
  4. Enter the question text and the answers, and mark the correct one. Set the time to answer and the scoring (normal, double, no points).
  5. Add more questions. They can be reordered and copied, and a long list can be loaded from Excel (“Import from Excel”).
{{ shot('q-types', 'Question types, from a single choice to a word cloud') }} {{ shot('q-editor', 'Quiz editor') }}

Questions can also be drafted with AI: use the “Create with AI” button in the catalog. Describe the topic and the number of questions, then check and edit the result.

Host a game

  1. Open the quiz and click “Start the game”.
  2. Show the host screen on a projector. It has the PIN and a QR code, and players’ names appear as they join.
  3. When everyone is in, click “Start”. After each question the correct answer and the votes are shown, then the leaderboard.
  4. When the game ends a report opens; you can download it as Excel.

Take a course

  1. Open “Courses” and find a course with the search or by subject.
  2. On the course page read the programme and click “Enroll”.
  3. Go through the lessons in order. A lesson can contain text, video, images, tests, assignments and problems from the archive.
  4. Tests are checked at once and can be retaken. Assignments are checked automatically too, or by the teacher if automatic checking is switched off.
  5. At the end of a lesson click “Learned”. When all required lessons are done, the course is complete.

Certificate

When you finish a course, a “Certificate” button appears on the lesson page. The certificate carries your name, the course title, the date, the teacher and a unique number such as OL26-000123-K7. The QR code leads to a verification page, so anyone can check that the certificate is genuine.

  • If your name is wrong, click “Change full name” and correct it.
  • The “PDF” button saves the certificate as a file for printing.

Create a course (for teachers)

Courses can be created by users with the “teacher” status. The platform administrator grants it: write to skarabaev@oshsu.kg and give your username.

  1. Open “Courses” → “My courses” → “New course”.
  2. Enter the title, the teacher’s full name (printed on the certificate), the subject and the course language. Click “Create”.
  3. In the “Programme” block add lessons. Modules are optional and only useful for big courses.
  4. Open a lesson and add blocks: text (Markdown and formulas are supported), video, image, file, test, assignment or a site problem.
  5. In “Course settings” write a description, check access and switch the certificate on.
  6. While the course is a “Draft”, learners do not see it. When it is ready, change the status to “Published” and save.

Learners and checking

The “Learners” page shows everyone’s progress and result and exports the table to Excel. If automatic checking of assignments is off, submitted work waits in “Review of work”: open a work, give a score and leave a comment.
